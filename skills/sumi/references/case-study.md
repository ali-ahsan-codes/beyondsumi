# First example: The Last Blossom

The 60-second Sumi-e prototype follows a samurai who returns to a ruined village, protects its final flowering tree, fails through anger, learns from a bending blossom, defeats a serpent, then supports the tree with his sword and scarf. Its defining idea is protection rather than conquest. This is an example, not the mandatory future story.

Historical project: The Last Blossom, a separate private prototype. The reusable skill does not depend on its owner-specific path or a running server.

Useful techniques: procedural brushwork, separate scarf/tree cloth chains, distance-based stride phase, two-link leg geometry, a pelvis-centered tumble, strike/contact cues, serpent collapse and dissolve, prop continuity and an ending with a visible act of care.

Limits to preserve honestly:

- This prototype's narration is the local Daniel English voice, not ElevenLabs or a cloned voice. The current production standard offers ElevenLabs, Voicebox, supplied audio or no narration.
- Its simple geometric character rig is a starting point; better anatomy, poses, grips and assets should be chosen for a new story.
- The source uses fixed-step cloth with a history-dependent update/warm-up on seek. That is not proof of deterministic arbitrary-frame cloth. Bake or checkpoint from a consistent initial state for reusable export.
- Older large narrative typography is superseded by the user's animation-plus-caption preference.
- Source inspection and earlier screenshots are not a fresh full playback or audio listening review. Re-review an actual derivative before delivery/publication.

The 29-second Vox-inspired adaptation is a separate engraved cut-paper treatment of the same plot. It uses ElevenLabs and new action/kneeling assets. Do not use its appearance as the definition of ink-wash Sumi-e or all Vox-inspired work.
