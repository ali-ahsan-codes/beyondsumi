**Sumi Light is the colorful brush-and-ink choice** in the active catalogue: Sumi Original, Sumi Light, Sumi Dark.

Use expressive Japanese Sumi brush silhouettes, translucent mineral-colored washes, broken bristles, feathered pigment, fibrous warm Washi and deliberate negative space. Black ink establishes structure. Selected color carries emotion, atmosphere or an important story object; the style does not require every palette color in every shot.

Read and apply the complete [shared brush-and-ink workflow](../brush-ink-workflow.md), profile `brush-ink-on-twos-v2`, and its [shot template](../../assets/templates/BRUSH-INK-SHOT.md). Light and Dark share their technical workflow; palette and color-specific effects are separate policies. Do not invent a different motion engine merely because the palette changes.

The character itself must be constructed from wet ink masses, pooling washes and broken brush edges. Use the [character prompt contract](../brush-character-prompts.md) and reject dominant pencil hatching, realistic cutouts or finely etched armour. Use the [recorded splash library](../ink-footage.md) for selected material reveals; do not hide anatomy beneath it.

## Storytelling purpose

Recommend Light for romance, children’s/general stories, YouTube documentaries and explanatory visual stories. Preserve the user-approved Light art and pose-sequence acting, selecting colours from the story/brief. Establish who/where/when, show cause and consequence, and direct emotional acting for romance/children’s stories or readable evidence/objects/maps for documentaries, as relevant. Keep factual claims supported; distinguish dramatized reconstructions. Large animated opening/topic text, compact stylish continuing captions and a considered ending must serve comprehension and retention. Do not import Dark's dominant splash/camera treatment or turn every documentary into the Last Gate's siege staging.

Choose a small story-relevant subset per scene. Use transparent pigment over luminous paper rather than opaque plastic color blocks. Composite washes with Multiply and intentional alpha/holdouts; pale bodies must occlude objects behind them. Let some paper remain between brush marks. A wet wash can have the shared inward pooling rim, diluted shoulder and irregular fibers.

For a story-appropriate impact, Light permits the supplied two-frame dark/light value exchange while an explicit **Cinnabar pigment mask preserves red**. Preserve only that authored pigment region, not arbitrary pixels that happen to be red. A motion-residue pass may use red or indigo, at up to 40% opacity, with broken edges and a short decay. These are optional action treatments; they do not belong automatically in a gentle touch, sleeping pose or grief beat.

## Technical profile and direction

- Output, camera, ink and particles: **24 FPS**.
- Character/internal motion sampling: **on twos: two-frame exposure units**, up to 12 changes per second. A drawing may hold for several units; actual drawing count is separate.
- Eligible contour boil: **6 or 12 exposure-aligned updates per second**, approximately 2–2.5 scaled pixels.
- Set a shot-specific drawing budget that covers anticipation, action, contact and recovery. Count every distinct pose/expression/atlas region; the former four-source/two-extreme cap is superseded.
- Layers: substrate/distant wash at `−500`, midground `−150`, complete hero/interaction `0`, foreground `+150`, with actual independent depth movement and protected support/contact.

The director selects the performance and camera from the approved story. A decisive action can use anticipation, a brief one- or two-frame dry-brush smear, checked follow-through and an impact recovery. A quiet scene can use one or two registered whole drawings, subtle eligible secondary motion and a deliberate hold. Do not turn the image budget into a reason to stretch anatomy or skip a required action; resolve the plan first.

Flow, Wash, Mist, Kasure, Sketch, Print, Scenic and Noir are compatible behavior vocabulary, not extra style choices. Previous four-method names remain historical techniques. Visible internal motion and evolving ink are required where the shot calls for life; color, particles and a slow camera move alone do not supply a performance.
