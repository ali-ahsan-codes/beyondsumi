# Sumo

The user's name for Sumi's **original samurai ink-painting method**, now preserved as a ninth selectable treatment. The method applies to other stories; samurai, a red scarf, English narration and a fixed duration are not compulsory. It is unrelated to the France Télévisions Sumo film, which remains a Print/action research reference.

## Movement and art

Use warm open paper, layered black/grey ink masses, expressive dry/wet edges and a restrained meaningful accent. Compose with space and contrast. Direct an intention, planted weight shift, committed gesture and lingering consequence. The original ink method's distinctive performance rests on brush-shaped poses, physical grounding, cloth delay, prop attachment, purposeful pauses and strong negative space. It is not a new name for one shared walking puppet.

Prepare original still artwork, separated body/limbs/cloth/props, ground, clean negative space and required key poses/views. Establish supports and grips in world coordinates before animating. Use capable local articulation for small changes, replacement drawings for changed silhouettes, and additional views or geometry for turns. Pin the cloth root and lag the free end behind actual body acceleration/wind; preserve release velocity and collision for props or petals. See [image-first workflow](../image-first-workflow.md) and [physics/review](../physics-and-review.md).

Economize with meaningful holds and reused separated art. Keep the decisive contact/release and recovery sufficiently sampled. A low drawing cadence is acceptable when intentional; sliding feet, disconnected grips, rigid cloth or an omitted action are not. Sumo may be more spare and pose-led than Wash's spatially fluid painted journeys, but direct that distinction from the story rather than forcing both into the same sequence.

Camera should expose a decision, effort or relationship. Use a held wide frame, prepared close view or motivated reveal; do not endlessly pan a finished painting. Transitions follow a continuing brush direction, prop, gaze or consequence. Keep atmosphere layered and subordinate to the performance. Avoid decorative splashes at every edit.

Prove the toughest required physical gesture with final art. Inspect consecutive frames of transformed support, grip, release and aftermath; watch the shot normally. The lab's planted reach and cloth-root overlay are a bounded mechanics lesson, not a finished fight or full category proof.
