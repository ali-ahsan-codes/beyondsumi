Use this standard for Sumi production and Teach Mode. It governs planning and review, not a claim that a particular film has passed. The three styles are Sumi Original, Sumi Light and Sumi Dark. Supporting character acting and cinematography must tell the story; Light/Dark share preparation tools; Dark uses a distinct broad-splash and camera-led motion language.

## Which budget applies

**Original:**30fps output/20 designed character states/s, action-driven source drawings and supported derived acting, with documented stillness. **Light/Dark:**24fps output with two-frame character exposure units, up to12 state changes/s and longer even holds as directed. All anatomy/props/cloth attached to a character use its same exposure state; camera/environment use the selected output clock. Historical source films remain untouched.

## Original: action-driven source budgets

Use the [scene-first workflow](scene-first-workflow.md). Select purposeful sources, checked cycles, supported derived motion and holds from the action. New Original uses20 designed character states/s at30fps with alternating two/one-frame units; source count is separate. Record contiguous absolute frame exposures, source hashes/regions, action IDs and hold reasons. Generate missing extremes/views/breakdowns when required, not arbitrary variations. Camera movement and repeated sources are not new drawings.

## Preserve whole-character geometry

Start from checked complete masters and whole interaction poses. Additional drawings must keep identity, proportions, camera, ground contacts and near/far limb order consistent unless the action deliberately changes them. Compare all planted paw, shoe and knee anchors after the final transform. One uniform translation cannot repair a source in which a dog and a girl have moved different amounts internally.

For small edits, use one whole-pair drawing as the baseline and change only the intended action. Check the result rather than assuming a precise prompt preserved it. Reject stretched bodies, sliding supports, changing hands or accidental extra limbs. Do not assemble independently generated limb fragments. A necessary new angle gets a complete alternate-view drawing.

Walking needs weight transfer and planted/moving phases. Sliding a frozen walking pose over a scrolling path is not a walk. A brief time-passage montage can hold distinct sizes/ages, but label and direct it honestly; it cannot replace a required performed journey. A death, rescue or acceptance is a one-time sequence, not a loop.

## Let narration and emotion direct attention

For each meaningful spoken or visual cue, record the subject, visible action, framing, angle, focal target, movement, start/end time and story reason. When a character becomes the subject, make them readable through composition, a supported closer view, a gentle push or a shift of attention. Do not zoom on every noun.

Establish enough geography before a close detail. A puppy-eye-level view can reveal traffic danger; a medium two-shot preserves the rescue relationship; a close hand/head view reveals acceptance; a wide view can make companionship or isolation legible. Hold on grief when stillness is the action. These are examples, not universal emotional meanings.

Camera motion must have a readable destination and settle. Distinguish a digital crop/zoom from a new perspective. Generate an alternate whole view if the shot needs an unseen side or different angle. Never rotate a flat illustration to claim a camera orbit. Preserve eyelines, screen direction, subject size relationships and ground continuity across reframing.

Separate foreground, acting plane and background where parallax helps. Keep paper texture fixed while painted scene layers move. Maintain occlusion: Multiply does not make pale body interiors opaque, so cars, branches and rain need appropriate whole-body holdouts or layer ordering. Test the actual composite during motion.

Ink focus can use a sharp subject with restrained, softer distant wash, not arbitrary camera blur that hides anatomy or turns ink into glossy photography. Captions occupy remaining negative space after the shot is composed; they must not dictate the same split layout in every scene.

## Keep scenes alive without decoration

Specify environment and response together. Visible rain has direction, depth, splashes, pooling and a shelter boundary. A car can throw water; a puppy can flinch or raise its head; contact can soften an expression. Environmental activity supports the action and continues through purposeful pauses. Do not replace acting with particles, line noise, constant scale breathing or repeated transitions.

Combine useful older mechanisms inside the selected style: ordered brush deposition, wet pigment evolution, checked acting and ink-painted explanatory relationships. For Light/Dark use the current shared profile; for Original preserve the first Sumi-e movement identity. These are techniques, not more styles.

## Review the real sequence

First inspect consecutive frames at movement/contact and compare master/pose overlays. Then play the complete bounded shot at normal speed. Ask:

- Is the required action visible when named, with a start, change and consequence?
- Do the extra drawings improve movement, or create pops, identity drift and foot sliding?
- Does the viewer's attention reach the intended subject without distracting camera motion?
- Are weather, depth, occlusion, material behavior and sound attached to actual events?
- Does a hold read as performance, or as waiting on an illustrated slide?

Add a drawing when a necessary state is missing; simplify camera or effects when they obscure it. Record failed criteria and fix them. A numeric asset budget, deterministic render or hash validation is not a visual pass. Preserve the approved wording and duration; return to the duration choice if the required action truly cannot fit.
