# Complete-character integrity before animation

This gate addresses the rejected film's reversed limbs and inconsistent assembly. A good IK distance does not establish correct anatomy. Automation checks records and provenance; visual inspection of the actual artwork remains necessary. Never promise a permanent zero-error guarantee.

Apply the current [on-twos workflow](brush-ink-workflow.md). Plan a shot-specific number of complete drawings sufficient for anticipation, action, contact and recovery. Count every intermediate, expression and alternate-view region even in an atlas; an intact interaction drawing counts once. Preserve required actions, approved narration and duration. Never deform anatomy or omit acting to meet the former four-source/two-extreme cap; that limit is superseded for new projects.

1. **Save the complete character first.** Use an original/generated full-body master with all limbs visible, consistent proportions, distinguishing marks and the actual needed facing/view. Add a side/three-quarter/rear reference only if the planned shot needs it. A face portrait or a collage of unrelated body parts cannot be the master. Record image path and SHA-256. Review the image before accepting it; do not automatically trust generated anatomy.
2. **Plan complete poses against that master.** Within the selected profile's budget, save each required source pose or view as a whole-character image. For all three styles, allocate the complete drawings and breakdowns the action requires; resolve missing intermediate or view requirements before production. Do not reuse a standing rig for an unsupported kneel, stride or turn. Annotate anatomical left/right separately from screen left/right, near/far limb order, shoulder/elbow/wrist, hip/knee/ankle, palm/thumb facing, foot/hoof direction, ground/contact points and support. Use species-appropriate anatomy; a quadruped hock is not a human knee.
3. **Review before motion.** Compare the full drawing with the master at equal body scale. Check limb count, proportion, joint bend, occlusion order, gaze, hand/paw orientation and support. Save an annotated overlay/contact sheet and a written observation with unresolved issues. Revise failed poses. Do not ask the beginner to perform these technical checks; the assistant handles them and shows a concise result when useful.
4. **Derive parts only if useful.** A rig may be cut from the checked whole character/pose. Record which parent drawing each piece came from and show the assembled overlay matching it. Independently generated limb atlases are prohibited. Do not mirror a whole character/hand without rechecking anatomical side, thumb/palm, near/far order, props and screen continuity. Do not rotate an upper arm/shin to imitate a missing perspective. Supply the necessary whole pose/view within the selected profile's budget, or resolve the direction before production.
5. **Review transformed poses and contacts.** Verify the rendered, scaled, rotated character against the reference, not just untransformed coordinates. Keep actual feet/support and grips authoritative. Reject unreachable IK, inverted joints, dislocated overlaps, limb stretching, skating and impossible release motion. Dense checks belong at contact and view changes; fewer drawings elsewhere are acceptable.
6. **Invalidate stale reviews.** Hash the master, pose images, annotated review images and mechanism source files. Changing any of them requires rerunning the relevant visual proof. A review of old artwork must not certify new pieces. `validate_shot_plan.py` checks these relationships and blocks the standard full-render command. It cannot infer whether an annotator was honest or whether the image is anatomically correct.

Apply [limb-motion review](limb-motion-review.md) to action transitions as well as the stable poses. A technically valid joint endpoint is not proof that the visible limb, sole or hand follows it.

For a method study without characters, declare `characters: []` and `character_free: true` with a reason. That does not certify later human or animal animation. At the proof stage, every used pose needs checked anatomy, identity, facing, contact and full-body evidence, plus actual clip/review/frame evidence. Keep exact approvals separate from technical checks; do not fabricate user approval.
