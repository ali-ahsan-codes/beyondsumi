# Sketch

Scratch-ink illustration driven by expressive gesture, relational acting and scale contrast. Negative space and selected loose lines emphasize intention. This is a performance method, not a blink added to a textured still.

## Binding movement identity

Read [the category contract](../animation-categories.md). Preserve Sketch's distinctive **gesture-and-response drawing**, rather than inheriting Flow's pigment transformations or Wash's default traversal. A gaze changes, a body prepares and acts, the other subject receives it and responds. Loose contours support that performance. The Circo reference shows full-body leaning/turning/raising arms as well as the larger creature's eye/head response.

**Build recipe:** author expressive extremes and needed intermediate drawings; hold stable landmarks while refreshing selected scratch contours. Pin hatching/hair to form, allow delayed secondary response, and compose negative space around the eyeline or reach. Use a close view only when there is a genuine new expression/detail to see. Movement can be calm or energetic according to the story; low drawing count is not permission for stiffness.

**Identity proof:** watch a complete exchange silently at normal speed. Intention and received response must read, including any required body change. Reject random line jitter, synchronized oscillator reactions, blink-only acting, and a shared generic rig merely rendered with hatching. Keep the detailed relational, support and redraw rules below.

**Observed in Chrome:** the [Circo reference](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/1053701644088588298/) places an enormous dark scratch-drawn creature above a tiny orange-coated child. Hair/garment movement and generous empty paper support the encounter. The evidence includes full-body and received response, not only eye animation. See the [motion reference recheck](../motion-reference-recheck.md).

**Our code adaptation:** separate body/face/hair parts, articulated joints, authored full-body and face-angle replacements, and stable contour variants. The pin's generator/source label does not establish software, exact cadence or soundtrack.

## Look and asset contract

Use quiet paper, confident dark silhouette and selective scratch contours. A restrained colour motif can identify the important person or relationship. Hatching follows form. Loose lines enrich a stable anatomy rather than changing it randomly. A small ground contact still needs physical support.

Prepare the performance's full-body extremes, head/neck views, eyelids, mouth poses, dedicated contact hands, hair/cloth roots and independent props. A hop, turn and raised-arm response need different body states; a rigid whole-image translation cannot provide them. Create alternate views when the subject changes perspective.

Choose the gaze relationship, size contrast and safe space before texture. Reuse drawing/physics helpers, but give Sketch its own performance, framing, cuts and action timing. Hatching another variation's identical rig is insufficient.

## Direct the encounter

Write each subject's intention and response: notice, approach, hesitate, attempt, receive, react and settle are useful concepts, not a compulsory sequence. Eyes may find a target before the head follows. A reach changes the hand/arm first, then the body's support as needed. A received response occurs after the other subject's action, not simultaneously by a shared oscillator.

A small figure can lean, hop, turn or step to make its meaning clear. For a hop, show compression, launch, airborne path and landing; for a turn, use another view or a designed drawing transition. Feet remain planted while thinking, unless an actual transfer and step is performed.

Large masses resist acceleration and settle more slowly than small details. The creature's head/neck response can lag the child's gesture, with eyelids/gaze focused on the correct target. Pin hair and cloth roots; free ends lag bodily acceleration and settle. Preserve local volume, eye position, teeth and fingertips.

An optional hand-drawn boil uses a small authored contour family with stable landmarks. Do not generate new random outlines every frame. Whole-image liquid displacement bends ground and face together and destroys the method.

## Camera, space and edit rhythm

Compose around the relationship: enough negative space for the look or reach to travel, and visible scale difference without losing the smaller subject's action. A held wider shot can preserve a complete exchange. A close view should reveal a tremor, blink, decision or contact, then a wider frame can restore its meaning.

The camera can support a turn or follow a small step, but continual approach steals attention from acting. Choose purposeful push-in/pullback when information or emotion changes. Use new detailed assets for close crops, not enlarged rough silhouettes.

Cuts can follow a gaze, body turn or drawn line. Do not impose generic brush wipes on every beat. Timing follows thought: hesitation, decisive movement and received response have different durations. Drawing exposure is an authored choice for the new scene, not a frame-rate claim inferred from sampled screenshots.

## Atmosphere and entrances

Keep environmental movement subordinate to the exchange. A small local breeze can move hair/cloth while the floor remains still. If a large shadow or creature enters, define where its mass comes from, what occludes it and when the small character notices. Do not fade it into existence solely because that is easy.

Scratch lines transform with the underlying geometry or are baked locally into each pose. A restrained dust or paper mark can indicate landing/contact after the event. Fog is optional and must not obscure the gaze corridor or substitute for unfinished anatomy.

## Narration, sound and captions

Provider choice follows [narration setup](../narration.md): ElevenLabs, Voicebox, supplied audio or none. Style direction never overrides that choice.

Preserve the complete approved script and measure the take before locking pauses. If expressive response and narration need longer, follow [script and duration](../script-duration.md): reuse authorization or ask the current user before changing runtime/script. Never remove lines to meet a preset.

Use the chosen narration provider or supplied take under [narration choices](../narration.md); skip voice production when narration is declined. Python synthesis can create original sparse tonal music, soft air, foot pressure, cloth/paper-like detail and restrained contact accents. Leave quiet around the received response; avoid generic boings or impacts on every line movement. This is proposed sound design, not verified audio from the source.

Caption actual phrases in stable high contrast, outside hands and gaze targets. Keep them away from the empty region carrying the emotional distance. Decorative word animation should not compete with the performers.

## Representative preflight and failure cases

Select proofs for the mechanics actually required by the current brief. Do not add a character, fight, camera move or transformation merely to satisfy an example below; a simple nature shot may need only its own release/contact and material proof. Explicit requests for silence, no narration or no captions override the defaults above.

Prove an entire short relational action using production assets, including a full-body change if the script requires one and the other subject's received response. Watch silently at normal speed: intention and response should be identifiable. Then inspect support, gaze, contact, landing, contour stability and close crops.

Fail for blink-only acting when full-body action is required, random contour jitter, floating feet, rigid hops, rubber turns, detached hair roots, wrong eye lines or identical choreography/camera to another style. The local blink lab is not a complete performance. Apply [motion direction](../motion-direction.md), [physics and review](../physics-and-review.md), [motion recipes](../motion-recipes.md) and the [production standard](../production-standard.md).

**Adaptable example:** a tiny traveller signals to a sleeping giant, steps back at its first movement, then raises a hand when the giant lowers its gaze. Another story may need two equally sized characters or no creature at all; preserve the relational logic, not the reference's cast or actions.
