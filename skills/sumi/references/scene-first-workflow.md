# Scene-first cinematic production — all three styles

Current shared workflow, updated24 October2026. Apply [Living Frames](living-frames.md) selectively in all three styles; retain complete-pose action preparation and the existing engine. Preserve the existing renderer, approved story/runtime, selected voice and each style's art identity. This extends the accepted Original scene-first method to Light/Dark; the example Marco scene is not a universal setting or pose budget.

## Before finished artwork

Concept → story → script → reuse/extend character bible → preliminary scene geography and camera purpose → narration/alignment → detailed shot breakdown → storyboard → rough timed animatic → cinematic layout → asset reuse/generation budget → contextual artwork/action poses → exposure sheet → animation → synchronized sound/compositing → integrated review.

First plan what the viewer must understand and the visible cause, action, reaction and consequence. Establish characters, motivations, setting, time, physical support, props and required actions. Preserve supplied wording and ask before changing approved runtime/content. Reuse existing approved voice rather than buying another take for picture-only changes. Use actual speech timing for the detailed shot schedule. Storyboards/layout marks and a rough timed preview precede finished image batches; written tables alone do not prove a sequence works.

Apply [cross-shot continuity](production-continuity.md) before prompting and across every edit.

Each shot records purpose, opening/closing state, narration cue, frame range, supported camera view and destination, character performance, screen direction/eyelines, set/prop ownership, light, contact, caption/title-safe area, transition and sound intention. A true new angle needs matching artwork; a moderate crop/push may reuse adequate resolution. Preserve neighboring-shot geography and scale. Do not rotate a flat front view into a side view or use camera motion to conceal missing acting.

## Compose first, derive layers second

Create/reuse an integrated shot master: subjects and their surroundings share perspective, scale, light, grounded support, shadows and occlusion. Furniture, touch, seated pairs, fighting contacts and walking-with-props belong to the same planned composition. Close views can simplify scenery when geography is established. Keep the master for comparison.

Use intact contextual panels where they carry the shot. Derive clean plates, characters, foreground masks or rigs from the coordinated master when independent motion helps. Record parent master, source hash/region, view/light/style, resolution and registration. Layering remains useful; independently invented people and backgrounds do not become integrated through compositing. Never erase pale garments as paper, cut required limbs, duplicate furniture/props or use Multiply as body transparency. Inspect observed gutters, every used panel and final framing; do not assume the generator obeyed a requested grid.

Before each image call choose: exact compatible reuse → supported local transform → checked source-derived rig → derive from master → purposeful pose sheet → new composition where necessary. Request only required views. Every pose has an action phase and contact/transition purpose. Validate sheet-cell resolution; use smaller batches when needed. Keep cached requests/settings/reference hashes where available, and inventory actual calls/retries/reused/derived sources. Do not claim measured cost/token savings without measurements.

## Distinct style contracts

| Style | Artwork and motion | Clock and sources |
|---|---|---|
| Original | Pure Japanese Sumi-e narrative surfaces; expressive emotional acting; full-frame Sumi Anime title/presentation. Clean edits and quiet paper/mist continuity; no animated splash/brush handwriting or action slashes. | 30fps output/20 designed character states per animated second, alternating one/two-frame exposures; action-driven sources or supported derived micro-acting, not20 new full-scene generations/s. Document intentional stillness. |
| Light | Colourful wet brush/ink subjects and world, story-selected mineral washes, documentary clarity, cause/effect, readable objects/evidence and explanatory framing. Purposeful local action ink and restrained material transitions. | 24fps output, paired character states with longer even holds; drawings chosen by action, not twelve new images/s. |
| Dark | Broad splash-built characters/world, black/grey and selective dark red; dramatic subject importance, faces/details, supported angle changes and stronger camera-led depth/reveals. Larger movement-linked brush/splashes with quiet reading intervals. | 24fps output, paired character states with longer even holds; independent camera/pigment each output frame where useful. |

Common preparation does not require identical performance, framing or effect intensity. Dark is not Light recoloured. Preserve Light's accepted treatment and story palette; do not turn it into Original's intimate porch/anime staging. All are story-directed YouTube films with a meaningful opening, compact stylish continuing captions and a deliberate ending.

## Animation and sound

Choose poses, checked cycles, supported rigging/secondary motion and holds by the action. Plan connected anticipation → contact/action → reaction → settling, including starts/stops and neighboring shots. Keep stride tied to ground travel, planted support fixed in world space, grips/props attached and outcomes persistent. Use additional matching drawings when sources cannot support a move. Avoid blind optical interpolation, incompatible pose crossfades and deformation through faces/grips. Camera, weather and pigment do not count as new drawings or substitute for acting.

Keep one output-frame event ledger for source exposures, character/camera cues, contact, local ink, scene transitions, narration and SFX. Sound intentions are planned with scenes; final effects follow actual animation. Retain separate narration, music, ambience and material/impact tracks. Duck music under speech, recover it when appropriate, protect intended silence and audible dramatic build/stop contrasts, and align effects with visible causes. Measure release against an unducked reference where needed, inspect peaks/fades and listen; meters alone do not judge emotional sound quality.

## Proof, review and scope

Propose a bounded generation batch. Prove one representative integrated scene and difficult action before bulk production; recheck final artwork after a rough proof. If a source fails, fix the smallest affected asset. Preserve old films and update only dependencies of the changed shot. Maintain deterministic frame evaluation and cached assets in the existing engine.

Inspect storyboard, complete source art, neighboring poses, normal-speed action, support/contact, camera/text clearance, style material, spoken cues, sound and the encoded export. Record unperformed checks; never prefill artistic/physics approval. Current helpers check file/timing evidence, not whether a scene looks right. A requested short proof remains a proof until reviewed. Reusable workflow edits alone authorize no film, voice, image, installation, upload or publication.

Environmental motion: use [the shared living-environment plan](living-environments.md) before rendering. Select local story-led motion or deliberate stillness, retain this style’s palette/cadence, and bind effects/masks to each actual source.
