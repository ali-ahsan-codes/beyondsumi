# Direct an animation treatment, not a skin

Read this before directing a Sumi film or comparison. Read [the three current styles](animation-categories.md), [complete-character integrity](character-integrity.md) and relevant dated source evidence. Sumi Original preserves its source-informed character; Sumi Light and Sumi Dark share the [brush-and-ink profile](brush-ink-workflow.md) with their separate palette rules. Older Sumi Brush, Living Ink, Drawn Acting and Sumi Graphic remain internal movement techniques. They do not prescribe one subject, rig, duration or shot list.

## Separate story identity from performance

Keep the same causal story, character identity and approved narration when comparing styles. Share utilities for time, rendering, audio, IK and caching. Direct staging, poses, facial acting, action curves, camera and transitions for the actual story. Light/Dark may reuse technical preparation and checked whole poses, but Dark must be redirected with larger splash-built forms and a more camera-led ink/depth journey; Original retains its preserved identity. A palette change is not evidence of newly authored acting. Small differences in easing, FPS, fog opacity or grain do not repair a missing performance.

Before asset production, write a brief motion treatment with observable decisions:

- **Acting:** what drives the performance—silhouette/line-of-action changes, replacement drawings, expressive close-ups, brush-shape transformation, or coherent layered depth? What poses and views are needed?
- **Time:** where does the viewer anticipate, accelerate, experience contact, and register the aftermath? Where is stillness intentional? Do not infer a reference's drawing cadence from a few sampled frames.
- **Space and camera:** what geography and support planes exist? Why is a wide, tracking shot, push, pull, tilt, detail insert or held camera appropriate? Distinguish subject movement from camera movement.
- **Transition:** what visible action, matching shape, moving occluder, change of depth, or motivated cut connects shots? What survives the transition?
- **Material and atmosphere:** how do fog, bristles, grain, cloth, petals, water or shadow move in this world, with what depth and causal relationships?
- **Sound/captions:** what events have audible material/mass, what carries the score, where does silence matter, and where can captions sit without hiding the action?

“Cinematic”, “smooth” and “wow” are desired outcomes, not usable animation specifications. Replace them with shot-specific decisions. A quiet close-up can be more effective than a constant camera move. Use zoom in/out when it changes information or emotion; do not add it merely to satisfy a checklist.

## Story and shot ledger

Read [script-duration.md](script-duration.md). Keep three inventories distinct: approved narration, any required displayed text, and indispensable visual beats. Map them to shots. Example fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Beat ID and consequence | What must be understood; what state changes |
| Speech/text IDs and exact selected wording | Prevent missing, duplicated or silently shortened content |
| Start/end world state | Location, support, gaze, held props, damage and relationships |
| Pose/asset needs | Actual views, hands, foreshortening and hidden surfaces |
| Performance and contact events | Preparation, contact, reaction, follow-through and persistent outcome |
| Camera/edit | Focal subject, trajectory, framing, cut reason and screen direction |
| Atmosphere | Depth, entrances, wind, occlusion and dissipation |
| Sound/caption cues | Linked events and final recording alignment |
| Review evidence | Hardest-shot clip, adjacent frames, constraints and remaining defects |

A beat is covered only when it is visibly performed or intentionally conveyed through an approved alternative. A bandage appearing on the next shot is not the same as a requested wrapping action. A narrator saying a rescue happened is not evidence that the rescue is visible. Match cuts and offscreen implications can be good direction, but cannot silently remove a required causal event.

Estimate duration using the actual language/delivery and action plan. Speech and action may overlap; do not add their durations blindly. Allow unvoiced time for impacts, suspense and the ending. One shared narration recording may be reused in a comparison when requested; preserve its words while placing phrases around each treatment's action. Identical narration does not require identical action timestamps.

## Prove the difficult motion first

Use the simplest proxy assets that expose the hard problem. Choose a representative passage for this specific treatment: a supported walk-stop-turn, reaching grip, physical cut, flower release, material morph, or fog reveal across depth. Include the planned camera and event timing. Play at normal speed, then inspect adjacent frames around contacts and cuts. Validate actual rendered transforms and silhouette extrema. Replace proxies with final art and check again; new artwork can introduce clipping, off-pivot grips and bad occlusion.

If a view change or detailed action cannot be supported by the current assets, first resolve it within the selected style's source budget. For Light/Dark, restage, choose a supported detail, simplify the visible motion, or plan a genuine story-motivated cut before requesting extra artwork. A new pose or view counts toward the current shot-specific drawing allowance; the old four-source/two-extreme cap is superseded. Allocate the needed checked drawings rather than silently removing a required action or substituting an unsupported rigid cutout pivot. Expose limitations before a whole-film render. The [physics guide](physics-and-review.md) gives specific tests and bounded helper code.

## Style and performance review

For a requested comparison, review each export against Original, Light or Dark's actual contract. A fixed-camera, monochrome diagnostic may help reveal action, support and contact hidden by colour or camera movement. It does not require Light and Dark to invent different motion for their shared system. Keep meaningful ink deformation visible and compare:

1. Acting and silhouette progression.
2. Temporal phrasing: anticipation, acceleration, held moments and settle.
3. Staging, camera and spatial reading.
4. How shots connect and how material transforms.
5. Environmental/secondary motion and its interaction with the action.

Light and Dark may share a performance and shot graph when appropriate; check their palette/material rules and the actual story-specific action. Original must retain its source-informed character. Reuse older stroke redraw, evolving ink mass, whole-pose acting and shape transformation where useful without turning them back into primary categories. Record observable strengths, defects and purposeful differences in concrete terms, not a numerical distinctness score.

## Sound and caption direction

Use local Python synthesis for original background music, ambience and effects by default, and the selected provider or supplied audio for requested narration. Build score structure around that film's tension and release. Derive effect onset from contact/release events; differentiate attack, impact and aftermath. A switch of instrument alone is not sound direction. Preserve voice intelligibility and useful silence. Specify material, envelope, spectral weight, distance and decay instead of claiming synthesized sounds are authentic recordings.

Captions keep the approved words and actual take timing. Their typography and presentation can fit the treatment, but legibility wins over rough ink, motion, or decoration. Use limited phrase groups, avoid faces/hands/contact, and retain a readable hold. Do not duplicate narration as large titles unless requested.

## Completion evidence

Use the production standard's full review gate on the actual final revision. A successful encode proves neither story completeness nor good animation. A static contact sheet cannot prove continuity. Unit tests cannot prove acting, and audio meters cannot prove listening. Record what was watched, frame-checked, measured and heard. Revisit changed shots and dependent sound, and do a final whole-film review after timing changes. Keep known material defects open; label an unresolved cut as a draft. Do not promise zero errors or treat skill documentation as pre-approval of future output.
