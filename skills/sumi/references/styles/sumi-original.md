## Current visual contract —22October2026

Every character, animal, prop and setting is rendered as **traditional Japanese Sumi-e narrative art**: expressive economical ink-painted forms, soft translucent tonal washes, paper texture, quiet negative space and composed landscape depth. Keep human faces, eyes, hands and gestures in the same Sumi-e language; do not replace the Sumi-e surfaces with generic cel-shaded cartoons, pencil sketches, etched comics, realistic cutouts or glossy3D characters. Anime-inspired title choreography, expressive performance and cinematic framing are welcome within the Sumi art treatment. Story requirements determine identity, clothing and setting; the Japanese art treatment is universal, not a requirement to relocate every story to Japan.

The user's “no brush and stroke” means **no animated brush-stroke drawing/reveals, action slashes, splash-built character transformations or decorative ink-spray transitions** in Original. The static art may naturally show the painter's ink handling; do not mistake that for permission to import Light/Dark's animated splash effects. Animate the painted people, cloth, expression, environment and story action. Choose clean scene edits, gentle paper/mist continuity and story-led camera framing instead. Preserve the historical prototype below as provenance, not as a rule overriding this current art direction.

## Full-frame presentation and Sumi Anime opening —22October2026

The current **Sumi Original** presentation is a **Sumi Anime treatment**, not a fourth catalogue style. Default to **full-frame16:9,1920×1080**: the painted scene fills the picture edge to edge. Natural paper/mist/negative space belongs inside that scene; avoid an inset slideshow, artificial letterbox bars, decorative picture borders, a permanent split layout or a separate subtitle strip. A later explicit aspect-ratio request overrides this default. Compose/generate scene art and supported camera views for the actual16:9 delivery and safe crop/overscan; never stretch a drawing or cut off needed heads, hands or feet to fill the frame.

**Continuing captions:** keep them in the lower picture, raised by about **10% of frame height above the usual bottom-caption position**. For planning, a conventional95%-height baseline lifted by10% gives an85%-height reference baseline:918px in a1080px picture. This records the user's relative placement; it does not mean a caption centred10% from the top or outside the video. Reflow a second line upward, retain side margins, and adjust composition where necessary to protect faces, grips and plot-critical props. Use compact readable phrases, a stylish restrained font from the liked/available family, and a deliberate soft paper or translucent ink-tone backing that contrasts with the actual shot. Keep consistent padding and subtle edge/shadow treatment; avoid oversized block captions or a large opaque permanent banner. Check at intended viewing size. No new font download/install is implied.

**Opening hook/title:** make a distinctive anime-inspired title mini-scene in the Sumi world. Start with a meaningful visual/emotional hook and prepared acting or camera movement, then resolve an attractive large English/script-language title using elegant calligraphic/serif or a suitable available Japanese-inspired display treatment, composed tracking, layered mist/paper depth and a story-led musical accent. Use clean type masks/opacity/scale/spacing and a readable steady hold; no animated brush handwriting, splash wipe or Dark-style impact treatment. A short emotional story may need a gentle lyrical title, while tension may justify a more dramatic reveal. The imagery, type composition, entrance and score should form Original's own opening identity rather than reuse Light's documentary splash or Dark's broad ink confrontation. Title size differs from the compact continuing captions. Preserve the exact approved wording/runtime, protect the first narration line and add Japanese writing only when the wording is supplied or verified/approved as needed. This is not permission to copy an existing anime title/logo.

Keep30fps output,20 designed character states/s with alternating one/two-frame exposure units, action-driven source counts and checked contextual shot/layer preparation. Smoothly align title, camera, character state and sound on the shared output-frame timeline. These are authored production directions; still examples, numerical placement and FPS do not prove actual animation quality.

## Purpose and drawing allowance

Recommend Original for richer painted character animation and expressive stories, including emotional, love and companionship narratives. Plan contextual scenes and matching cinematic views, then choose new poses, checked cycles, supported derived motion and holds by the action. Count distinct drawings, repeated states, image calls and output exposures separately. Original now uses20 designed states/s at30fps, usually one/two-frame alternating exposures. These states may be whole drawings or supported derived acting; a camera/light-only change is not a character state. Stillness may deliberately hold longer with a meaningful reason. No count or rate alone proves acting.

## Baseline identity

The first visible delivery was **56 seconds**, with seven scenes, music, effects and on-screen narrative but no spoken narration. The earlier recovered source consists of `index.html`, `style.css` and `film.js`, with the Web Audio score embedded in the JavaScript.

The later **60-second narrated revision** is preserved separately. It adds eight local Daniel voice clips, heavier captions, a literal serpent death and revised physics. Do not silently substitute that revision's narration or climax when referring to the first film.

The original plot is an example: a samurai tries to fight a storm represented by an ink dragon, then gives his sword and red scarf a protective purpose beside a fragile tree. Samurai imagery and combat are not requirements for future Original stories.

## What the source actually does

| Element | Recovered Original implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Paper | One stable offscreen paper layer with seeded grain and fibers, drawn under each frame; additional grain runs through the finished image. |
| Palette | Warm off-white `#eeeadd`, dark ink `#242b29`, muted red `#a34537`, with olive and green-grey landscape/clothing shades and warm highlights. |
| Brush | Several offset bristle paths, variable weight and density, irregular specks, translucent mountain washes and clipped dry garment texture. |
| Composition | Layered mountains, hills, ground and foreground; strong silhouettes, tree/figure scale, mist, grass, leaves and open areas arranged around the action. |
| Characters | Authored Canvas paths and articulated geometry with local pose parameters, cloth and props. The prototype does not use generated whole-pose image sequences. |
| Acting | Travel, reach/readiness, sword phrases, airborne movement, fall/recovery, attention to a petal, tree support and a settled ending. |
| Camera | Pans, pushes, pullbacks and brief impact shake tied to the scene. |
| Cuts | Brief paper veils around scene cuts: a 0.24-second approach and retreat, with up to 0.88 overlay opacity. |
| Cadence | Browser `requestAnimationFrame` with elapsed-time motion. No fixed drawing FPS or encoded output FPS was specified. |
| Sound | Original inline synthesized wind, tones, bells, low drums and swishes; no speech loader in the 56-second baseline. |

The original brief requested a predominantly black/white/grey appearance with very limited accent colour. The implementation includes subdued green/olive shades. Preserve that distinction between intended palette and actual source. A measured 85/15 colour split, fixed 30–40% negative space, pure-white background, 8–12 drawing FPS or mandatory splash-transition library is not established by the original source.

Original's material character comes from brush marks, layered washes and paper. The source does not implement a full wet-ink fluid simulation. Its motion is continuous procedural action; reducing redraw rate or applying later methods does not by itself preserve its performance.
