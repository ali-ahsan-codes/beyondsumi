# Light/Dark character material contract

The whole character must look made from brush-loaded wet pigment, not a detailed sketch placed over an ink-painted background. This applies to **Sumi Light and Sumi Dark only**. Preserve Original's source-informed Sumi-e art identity and its historical files.

Follow [scene-first cinematic production](scene-first-workflow.md). Plan the integrated character/environment composition, supported view, light, contact and camera destination before any extraction. A reusable identity/model sheet can guide that shot; it does not replace the shot's layout. Then apply this material direction:

> Complete [CHARACTER] in [SUPPORTED VIEW AND WHOLE POSE] within [COORDINATED SHOT/LOCATION], with [CORRECT LIMB/SUPPORT/GRIP DETAILS], shared perspective/light and [PROP/SET OWNERSHIP]. Japanese wet brush-and-ink painting: form body, clothes and surroundings from broad confident calligraphic ink masses and translucent washes, varied brush pressure, pooled pigment, soft capillary bleeding and broken bristle edges. Integrate story-appropriate splash satellites into silhouette/motion accents; keep face, palms, joints and soles readable. Large connected value shapes carry anatomy. Leave deliberate paper gaps and negative space. [STYLE PALETTE]. Retain the complete contextual master; derive registered character/foreground/plate layers only if the planned animation needs them. No independently invented cutout/background pair.

For **Light**, replace palette with: “Carbon-black structural strokes and a restrained [STORY-SELECTED MINERAL COLORS] wash; luminous paper and transparent pigments. No plastic shading.” For **Dark**, also add “Oversized loaded-brush masses form the entire figure, broad calligraphic anatomy, substantial splash-built silhouette breakup, large pooled black areas with deliberate readable white cuts; reduce microdetail further than Light. Neutral black, white and grey base; selective deep dark-red pigment for story-significant blood, eyes or character/scene details, with explicit placement. No warm paper, sepia, other hues or global red tint.”

Negative/material exclusions where supported:

> No pencil/graphite drawing, engraving, etching, cross-hatching, thin scratchy sketch outlines, comic-book contour tracing, finely rendered realistic armour plates, photographic skin, 3D render, or a finished realistic cutout merely surrounded by ink splatter. No decorative splashes across eyes, grips or supporting feet. No missing/reversed limbs.

Do not use “graphic novel,” “sketch,” “highly detailed realistic warrior” or similar conflicting descriptors as the dominant Light/Dark prompt. Controlled fine accents may describe an eye or hand; they must not replace the broad wet-ink construction. Splash language describes the character's actual material, not permission for unreadable anatomy or a full-screen effect in every shot.

Before producing more poses, inspect the complete master against a background sample at comparable scale. Check whether the torso/limbs read as broad pigment masses at thumbnail size and retain coherent joints at close size. Reject a realistic or pencil-like character on a brush background even if the palette matches. Save a material observation alongside anatomy review. Use a new proper master when needed; grayscale/recolouring and edge splatters cannot repair fundamentally wrong art construction.

For action poses, retain whole-character identity and the [limb-motion review](limb-motion-review.md); brush economy does not justify distorted joints. The prompt is a direction template, not a guarantee of model output or proof that any new character has already been generated.


## Stronger stroke-built silhouettes

User wants Light/Dark characters to read as moving brush strokes, not clearly traced realistic warriors with ink effects added around them. Build head, torso, garments and limbs from economical connected calligraphic masses, varied pressure and purposeful paper gaps. Reduce armor/skin microdetail and continuous hard outlines. Preserve identity through major shape, posture, costume masses and selected facial marks. Keep enough structure for facing, grips, feet and story actions to read; expressive abstraction is not permission for confused anatomy.

This strengthens the existing Light/Dark material rule. The detailed12-frame walking study establishes an image-sequencing approach the user likes, not the final brush-material target. Preserve Original's separate Sumi-e identity. Before more production, approve the complete character's brush construction at actual shot scale alongside the setting, then carry that construction consistently across the connected action sequence.


## Consistency across the complete film

Use the same broad wet-brush construction in openings, commanders, animals, background figures and endings as in the principal action. Carry major costume/value shapes and pigment scale from the approved master through every view. Do not alternate detailed etched figures with abstract wet-ink fighters.

Dark shares checked source preparation, but requires a distinct source silhouette and camera/splash grammar: larger connected black masses, forceful broken bristle edges, pooled ink, splash-shaped silhouette breakup and deliberate white cuts. In darker stories, use darker value staging and more emphatic motion-linked local splash, while preserving readable anatomy, faces, grips and ground contact. Add this treatment to the source drawings, not only an effect over a realistic character. Neutral base and selective purposeful dark red remain required; quiet scenes still need breathing room.


For Dark camera-led production, decide shot size, supported view/angle, emotional focal detail, near/far order and camera destination before writing the image prompt. Request matching complete expression/action drawings for that specific composition. A face close-up needs the planned gaze and emotional progression; a new low/high/profile/over-shoulder view needs a complete matching source view, not a front-view crop pretending to change perspective. Keep same-view action-sheet registration stable; prepare new view groups for perspective changes. Link to the [Dark cinematic direction](styles/sumi-dark.md) for shot and synchronization choices.
