## Dark stories: splash-built forms and camera-led storytelling

Recommend Dark for dark stories, dread, conflict, mystery and dramatic consequences. The common sheet/crop/sync process is preparation, not permission to reuse Light's performance and recolour it. **Dark must have its own larger-scale brush and splash construction and more prominent cinematic camera language**, inspired by the supplied Marco Polo reference. Do not claim a new reference-film study unless performed.

Build entire characters, animals, objects and scenery from broad loaded-brush masses, pooled black, coarse broken bristles, substantial splash-shaped silhouette edges and deliberate white cuts. Use larger strokes within the source drawings, not just splatter overlays around a detailed figure. Keep identity, facing, expressive eyes, grips and load-bearing joints readable. Use purposeful dark red in subjects, eyes, blood when the story calls for it, or a meaningful scene motif; no global red tint or gratuitous gore.

Plan a camera journey for each beat: establish the ink world, travel along a motivated silhouette/landscape or prop, pass through supported depth, settle on an expressive detail, then release into the next composition. Use stronger pushes/pullbacks, tracking, subject-following pans and foreground ink occlusion where the story supports them; generate matching views for real perspective changes. A moving crop is not a 3D orbit. Combine camera, brush formation, large local pigment evolution and required pose changes into one performance. Maintain grounded contacts; camera motion must not disguise missing hands/feet or skipped actions.

Large splashes may form a subject or carry a meaningful transformation between shots. Distinguish subject formation, local motion residue and scene transition. Give the viewer a readable subject/phrase hold between major events. Avoid recurring fast left wipes, constant full-frame splashes, random shake or uninterrupted zooming. Quiet dread can hold with tiny pigment movement before a stronger camera/reveal beat. End on the story's consequence, not another arbitrary splash.

## Cinematic character direction — locked22October2026

Dark puts **cinematography, character importance and dramatic timing first**. Keep the accepted brush/splash palette, preparation, captions, sound and cadence. This refinement strengthens Dark's shot design; it does not change Light/Original or automatically rerender an approved film.

Choose the emotional subject and camera plan before creating images. For each beat, name what the viewer must notice, the exact story/voice cue, shot size, supported angle, focal detail, camera destination and quiet reading hold. Build a purposeful progression where useful: establish geography → medium character performance → face/eyes or hand/prop detail → action/splash punctuation → reaction or consequence. This is a palette of choices, not a fixed sequence for every scene. Faces and expressions deserve prepared drawings when they carry a turning point; a close-up must reveal the character's emotional change, not simply enlarge a neutral master.

Use story-motivated close-ups/extreme details, wider reveals, low/high views, profile/three-quarter views, over-shoulder framing, controlled push-ins/pullbacks and tracking. A low view may give a subject importance, a high view may expose isolation, a face shot may make dread readable; choose from the scene, not a mandatory angle list. Prepare matching complete masters, expression/action breakdowns, interacting figures and background views for the chosen lens-like composition/perspective. For a true angle change, generate a complete supported alternate view; enlarging or rotating a front drawing is not a new angle. Keep eyelines, screen direction, proportions, support and shared contacts coherent. Retain the full source even when deliberately framing a face or hands tightly.

Treat a **splash shot** as a composed dramatic image: a broad ink mass can reveal an important face/figure, briefly obscure a cut, follow force through an action, or become the next meaningful shape. Record its origin, focal target, reveal direction, peak and decay, leaving the character/action readable afterwards. Keep Dark's large source brushwork and camera-led depth; do not add a splash between every phrase or cover missing acting.

**Smooth synchronization:** plan source exposures, camera framing/settle, local splash, narration phrase and material sound on one output-frame timeline. Anticipation precedes release/contact; the visual cause and sound impact share the intended frame. Maintain paired character states at24fps with sufficient intermediate complete drawings for the action, longer even holds where directed, and camera/environment updates each output frame where needed. Use eased starts/stops and intentional settles; check registration, focal scale and audio continuity across joins. Smooth timing does not mean crossfading incompatible poses, washing out on-twos acting, floating feet or hiding a position jump in ink. Inspect the neutral action and final composite at normal speed and around the joins; add matching drawings or simplify the move when synchronization fails. Do not promise smoothness solely from FPS or a written plan.

## Neutral base, selective dark red

| Role | Reference value | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | White with neutral-grey fibers; no warm/sepia tint. |
| Structure | `#111111` | Neutral carbon black. |
| Dilution | `#5C5C5C` | Equal-channel grey; other neutral values allowed. |
| Dark-red accent | `#7A1622` recommended | Blood, eyes or a meaningful character/scene detail; explicit localized pigment/mask and story reason. |

Use a consistent selected dark red for a film. The bundled helpers accept muted red with red channel 64–180 and at least 1.6 times each other channel; other hues remain excluded. This is a palette check, not a pixel-level placement detector. Describe and inspect the actual accent regions, continuity and decay. Keep most scenery, character masses, captions and effects neutral. Do not turn the whole screen red or add blood to a nonviolent action. No gold, jade, indigo, peony, sepia or colored paper. A story without these red details may stay entirely neutral.

Neutral pigment can use Multiply with alpha/holdouts; pale faces, hands and bodies still occlude backgrounds. Preserve midtones, diluted shoulders, pooling rims and irregular fibers. Grayscale is not permission to crush all values into black.

An optional impact value exchange may preserve only the authored dark-red accent mask; the rest exchanges neutral structure/paper. Derived ghosts and local splashes are normally neutral, with dark red only when justified by the associated accent or impact. Follow the shared timing/opacity limits. The [recorded splash library](../ink-footage.md) supports this palette; use meaningful restrained scene changes.

## Direction, animation and type

- Output/camera/environment: **24fps**; complete character state on twos, up to **12 changes/s**. Count distinct drawings separately.
- Plan enough complete poses for anticipation, action, shared contact, weight transfer and recovery; no fixed source cap.
- Make local action ink clearly visible and somewhat larger where it helps: step drags, arrow trails, sword arcs and impacts. Preserve faces, grips, support and captions. Keep quiet intervals and separate these accents from scene transitions.
- Give camera travel, changing depth, large brush formation and subject acting a shared beat plan. Dark emphasizes camera/splash animation more than Light, but a moving flat card is insufficient for a required character action.
- Give the opening/story title a prominent attractive animation and composed scene. Keep ordinary narration captions compact, stylish, legible and proportionate, with suitable brush/ink backing. Finish with a deliberate consequence and sound decay.

The same method covers humans, animals, fights, wars and quieter stories, with different action budgets and staging. Use impact holds, shake and broad splashes only when the story benefits; gentle contact and grief may need stable framing and restraint. Original retains its own Sumi-e identity.

## Horror opening

When the approved story is horror, compose its own unsettling opening scene/background, prominent readable topic title and synchronized story-specific sound. Use meaningful localized dark red as appropriate to the horror brief. Establish the threatening image or sound before its payoff; do not recycle a Light/Original opener, globally tint red or force non-horror Dark stories into horror. Retain the neutral ink base and readable faces, midtones and captions.
