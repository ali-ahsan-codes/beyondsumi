# Noir

Black/white-led painted cinema with selective red, pink or yellow, fluid Wash-family acting and strong negative space. The current colour/movement contract below takes precedence over a rigid binary interpretation.

## Binding movement and colour identity

Read [the category contract](../animation-categories.md). Noir uses **Wash-family fluid painted action** inside strong black/white staging. Red is available, with **vibrant pink and yellow selectively available for characters, costume, light or meaningful effects**. Choose clear colour roles for the story; all accents need not appear together. This supersedes any older binary-only or red-only interpretation. Dark/light mass remains the foundation, while softness, grey depth and bleeding edges may support it.

**Build recipe:** design a readable silhouette performance, attach highlights/accent masks to the actual changing anatomy, and render local ink edges and atmospheric depth around it. Use a reveal through darkness, a coloured moving sleeve/face, continuous cloth lag, and an action-led sweep or clean cut where motivated. Pacing can alternate suspense, fluid movement and still aftermath. Do not turn it into repeated freezes, title cards or a mechanical translation because of its graphic art.

**Identity proof:** the selected action must read both as an evolving silhouette and as a material performance with accents restored. A dark frame can conceal information temporarily, then must reveal the decisive gesture or consequence. Check highlight/colour registration and visibility through any wash. Reject neon colour everywhere, rigid binary silhouettes, accents detached from anatomy, or a recoloured copy of a complete Wash film. Shared physics and a movement family are intentional; repeated staging and timing across every beat are not.

## Value design and assets

Plan two large value families before interior detail. Carve highlights to clarify a face, sleeve, hand or blade. Separate overlapping dark subjects through staging and negative space. An accent signals a specific change, not decoration. Soft ink edges and grey atmospheric depth are welcome while the dominant dark/light structure stays readable. Inherit Wash-family movement deliberately; do not flatten its material into rigid cutouts.

Prepare foreground masses, clean environment, layered silhouettes, selective highlight shapes, independent props and anticipation/contact/recovery drawings. A charging body needs changing legs, torso and cloth, not a rigid full-body translation. New perspective requires new artwork or geometry. Hide interior detail in a test: intention and decisive contact should still read.

Reuse shared deterministic/physics helpers, but direct Noir's own performance, camera, edit and transition logic. Enlarging another variant's frame slightly is not new cinematography.

## Subject performance and consequence

Anticipation changes the silhouette. Acceleration carries a readable action path; contact establishes a new state and recovery gives it meaning. A short smear can link designed extremes but cannot replace a missing contact pose. Holds serve readability; avoid an impact that appears accidentally frozen. Choose timing from the story and speech.

Weapon movement belongs to stance, torso, shoulder, elbow and wrist. The grip remains around its handle and the target reacts at the blade's actual contact. A released object inherits velocity before falling and settling. If divided, derive fragments from the same mask/geometry along the shown cut plane; the new state persists. Do not spawn unrelated confetti or revive a removed subject.

Highlights transform with anatomy. Dark regions cannot excuse missing limbs, floating support or duplicate props. Simplifying appearance does not remove cause and effect.

## Camera and edit rhythm

Use selective withholding and reveal. A close view of bracing feet or tightening fingers can build anticipation; a wider composition then exposes their purpose. A short lateral movement follows a charge through value shapes. A cut through a large black/white form can shift attention while preserving direction and location.

Settle the camera when a contact needs to be read. Tie limited decaying shake to an actual event. A push-in or pullback must reveal new information or emotional scale, supported by prepared assets. Quiet, a burst and aftermath can form a phrase, but they are not compulsory equal-length phases.

A motivated value inversion can punctuate a turn, sparingly. Avoid repeated full-frame flashes and high-frequency flicker. A slash transition requires directional continuity and narrative cause. Graphic type belongs only where the prompt needs a title/label; it must not interrupt every narrated beat.

## Atmosphere and entrances

Wind/rain use selected carved marks, consistent direction and depth roles. A mass can occlude/reveal a subject instead of fading everything from transparent. Make emerging silhouettes separate from the background before their gesture matters.

Keep highlights, shadows and masks in the correct coordinate frame. Dust/smoke needs an event source, travel and settling state. Hard edges do not justify particles appearing before contact or arbitrary fog obscuring a weak drawing.

## Narration, sound and captions

Provider choice follows [narration setup](../narration.md): ElevenLabs, Voicebox, supplied audio or none. Style direction never overrides that choice.

Preserve the complete approved narrative and visible causal events. Measure the take before setting suspense and impact windows. Follow [script and duration](../script-duration.md): reuse existing authorization or ask the current user before altering runtime or removing/rewriting lines.

Use the selected narration route; skip voice production when narration is declined. Python synthesis can create original percussion, dry swipes, tight contacts and low settling tails. Silence can emphasize a decision or consequence. Separate swish, collision and collapse rather than placing one impact on every cut. These are proposed sound choices, not verified source audio.

Use bold, stable phrase captions with quiet contrast. Optional vertical lettering needs a separate narrative role, correct language and no source-brand imitation. Keep words away from acting details and the value shape carrying a transition.

## Representative preflight and failure cases

Select proofs for the mechanics actually required by the current brief. Do not add a character, fight, camera move or transformation merely to satisfy an example below; a simple nature shot may need only its own release/contact and material proof. Explicit requests for silence, no narration or no captions override the defaults above.

Prove the hardest silhouette action with production assets: anticipation, full path, contact, reaction and persistent aftermath. View without interior detail, then with highlights. Inspect normal speed and adjacent frames around contact, division and release.

Fail for unreadable black-on-black forms, detached highlights, a slash hiding missed contact, overlong freezes, premature reaction, duplicate props or a complete recoloured shot graph. Shared Wash-family mechanics are appropriate when separately directed for Noir. The split lab is not a finished fight. Apply [motion direction](../motion-direction.md), [physics and review](../physics-and-review.md), [motion recipes](../motion-recipes.md) and the [production standard](../production-standard.md).

**Adaptable example:** a climber's hand braces in darkness, then a widening reveal shows a companion below; the rescue uses visible support and a quiet recovery. Invent another construction for another prompt. Noir does not require swords, red text or an advertising ending.
