# Whole-character action sheets

Default to planning animation step by step before generating images. Use complete, matching action drawings as the foundation for walking, hand gestures, fighting and other movement, rather than waiting for a standing rig to fail. Plan anticipation, key action/contact, weight transfer and recovery before animating. Use a relevant motion reference when the movement is uncertain; otherwise reason from anatomy and inspect the result. Reference stills are research, not artwork to ship. Do not claim reference study that was not done.

Preserve character identity, clothing, prop ownership, drawing scale, camera view and palette. Generate/draw complete poses, not independent limbs. Register the pelvis/body anchor and ground baseline across drawings. Inspect the full sheet before cutting cells or sequencing it. A matching face alone does not establish a usable action sheet.

For walking, check alternating near/far legs, contact, passing and opposite contact. Reject sheets that repeat the same leading leg, keep a wide combat stance, reverse knees/toes, detach hips or let feet trail behind the body. Face, torso, hips and feet must support the travel direction. Prefer short steps and restrained whole-body advance when the story calls for controlled motion; stronger actions need their own suitable poses. Small translation is a stylized staging choice, not proof of grounded locomotion. Coordinate planted feet with travel and explicitly disclose any visible skating.

Use enough whole drawings for the shot. Two or three can suit a limited gesture; a walk cycle or complicated action may need more. Count every pose separately even when several are generated in one sheet. Use the selected clock: Original30fps/20 designed character states per animated second, with action-driven sources and supported derived motion; Light/Dark 24fps with even-frame holds. Reuse only checked cycles and report their true source/exposure counts. Do not interpolate limbs into unsupported views or claim twelve new drawings per second merely from output cadence.

For kneeling, rising, reaching, gripping, striking and falling, create action-specific sequences with credible support and complete silhouettes; a walking sheet does not certify them. Review entry and exit as well as the middle. Keep meaningful story actions and approved timing.

Before whole-film rendering, inspect the exact final-art sequence at normal speed and adjacent poses, plus full-body neutral-camera evidence. Apply the existing limb-motion and physics gates; pose sheets are not an exemption. Fix source drawings when alignment fails rather than hiding the error with crops, dust or camera movement. Record unresolved anatomy/contact problems and unperformed playback checks.

Formation follows the story. For the current Last Gate correction the user requests one leader ahead and two followers behind, staggered in depth and step phase. This is a project staging instruction, not a requirement for every film.


## Complete-scene frame design

When multiple characters interact or parts-based motion causes alignment failures, design the whole scene as a sequence of complete images before animation. Plan each frame's poses, support/contact, hand/weapon ownership, relative positions and action progression. Lock the camera, background, character identity, palette and formation unless the direction intentionally changes them. A group walk can be drawn together rather than assembling three independent puppets; fighting must coordinate attacker, defender and contact in the same frame plan.

Generate/render distinct full-scene drawings from a shared scene master with precise incremental action instructions. Keep earlier and later frames available for continuity comparison. Check actual outputs: generators may ignore pose changes, repeat the same leading leg, alter faces/weapons or repaint static scenery. More images alone do not guarantee fewer errors. Repair faulty frames before claiming a final-ready sequence; retain bounded tests with honest limitations for comparison.

For this user's requested walking study, create12 separate whole-scene images: standing, small lift, passing, contact, weight transfer, opposite passing/contact, next step and settle; one leader and two followers. Twelve is the current scene budget, not a universal limit for every future scene. Other actions need their own ordered breakdowns. Do not label a single twelve-cell sheet as twelve independently generated images, or duplicate one image to claim twelve drawings.

Sequence reviewed images with explicit exposure counts on the selected output clock; the historical Light study below uses24fps. Use two-frame exposure units: a drawing may hold for 2, 4, 6, 8 or more even frames as acting requires. A constant four-frame hold permits 6 changes/s; two permits 12. Mixed holds are valid; report actual counts rather than saying every image is held exactly twice. Preserve the user's desired movement pace and disclose drawing/exposure counts. Source-image scene animation is compatible with Sumi's approved still-image/code workflow; it does not authorize external AI video generation. Existing visual/physics gates remain, and numeric geometry claims require measurements of the actual depicted poses rather than the obsolete puppet rig.


## Default sequence, then optional cutout animation

Plan the action before image generation: start pose, anticipation, incremental movement, contact, response and recovery/settle as applicable. For fighting, plan both participants together: attack path, block/contact, reaction and recovery, with weapon ownership and ground support maintained. For hands, plan shoulder/elbow/wrist/fingers and any object contact together. Non-human actions use their own meaningful phases rather than a literal human gait template.

For Light/Dark, use about five or six complete images as a starting budget for a simple action. New Original adaptations choose source drawings by the action and use20 designed character states/s at30fps; compatible derived micro-acting can supply states. This is neither a cap nor a mandatory minimum; fewer can serve a small movement and more may be necessary for complex interaction. The earlier twelve-image walking study is an accepted direction for the method, not a fixed source count for every scene.

First inspect and synchronize the whole drawings to establish the actual movement, then optionally cut complete figures, objects or connected parts from those checked images for controlled animation. Keep each cutout linked to its source pose; overlay it on that complete drawing to verify registration, silhouette, joints, grip and near/far order. Use cuts only where the available poses support the intended motion. Do not invent hidden surfaces, pull limbs out of alignment or stretch a single standing image into an unsupported action. Preserve an intact whole-image sequence as the comparison when cutting introduces defects.

Synchronize poses with story beats, narration and sound contacts after the motion reads correctly. Camera, composition, transitions, captions, atmosphere and dramatic sound remain part of the directed scene; this method does not replace them. User approval of a method or a particular visual result is not certification of every contact or future action. Retain review of actual adjacent frames and playback, and disclose unresolved defects.


## Connected momentum, not isolated action loops

Design an action phrase across its entire sequence before generating the images. For example: walk → gather weight → takeoff → airborne sword preparation → swing/contact → landing/compression → recovery. Carry facing, direction, speed, balance, prop ownership and consequences through the joins. Do not reset the body into a neutral stance between connected actions or splice unrelated walk/jump/strike loops. Plan interacting characters, collision and reaction in the same frames. This applies to human and non-human action with suitable anatomy.

Choose enough complete drawings to establish changes in silhouette, support and momentum. Use checked cutouts, repeated cycles, camera/depth movement or small supported articulation as economical in-betweens only after those drawings define the path. They fill supported gaps, not missing takeoff, contact, landing or other required beats. Never claim measured token savings simply from a lower image count; minimize unnecessary generation while preserving the action. Keep Original exposures on its30fps whole-frame timeline and Light/Dark exposures paired at24fps and coordinate camera/environment independently.

For Light/Dark, motion also carries pigment: short dry-brush trails following a step or cloth direction, tapered ink slashes following the actual sword arc, and local splash/dispersal at contact or landing. These are movement accents within the scene, distinct from scene-change transitions. Vary strength by action and leave quiet intervals. Never blanket every frame, cover support/grips, imply blood where the story does not call for it, or use a splash to conceal broken motion. Read brush-character-prompts.md and ink-footage.md for actual material and reference-based transition direction.


## Locked common method — all three styles

The user approved the recent complete-pose/sheet-and-crop approach as the default for **Sumi Original, Sumi Light and Sumi Dark**. Apply it to human gestures, walking, fighting, wars, animal action and quiet storytelling. This locks a production method, not the same poses, staging, source count or action intensity for every story. Original keeps its preserved Sumi-e drawing and scene identity; new Original adaptations use this preparation without changing historical films.

Prefer one large image sheet per related scene group or connected action, containing ordered complete poses or complete interacting scenes. Plan the action before generating that sheet; do not treat one image as a limit for the entire film. Multiple matching images are also valid when the sheet would make each drawing too small. A sheet may cover a connected action or planned shot variants; different perspectives/compositions need their own supported drawings and directed cuts. Cropping alone does not create an unseen angle or intermediate action.

Before animation, inspect each cell at usable delivery scale. Record the source hash, board dimensions, cell/ROI coordinates, original registration anchor and ground baseline, exposure order and counts, character/weapon ownership, shared contact and excluded cells with reasons. Retain full limbs and props, useful padding and the intact sheet for comparison. Reject repeated non-progressing poses, missing swords, extra arms, wrong joints, clipped bodies and neighboring-cell fragments. Adjust cell bounds/transparent margins while preserving anchor and scale; do not resize each tight crop independently and create body jitter. If a cell cannot carry its required action, repair or replace that drawing before relying on it.

Synchronize the checked whole poses first. Then use complete-figure or verified connected-part crops, supported small movement, scene scrolling, camera/depth and economical held exposures to finish the performance. Keep the original whole-scene sequence available to detect cutting regressions. A sheet with six distinct poses is six drawings in one generated file; report generated files, accepted/rejected drawings, repeated exposures and derived motion separately. Five–six drawings is a Light/Dark starting allowance for simple action, not a cap. Original uses action-driven sources and20 designed character states/s at30fps; several drawings may share a sheet.

For Light/Dark, make the approved action accents clearly readable at normal playback: broader step drags, tapered arrow/sword trails, and stronger local impact splashes where appropriate. Start slightly larger than the recent subtle accents, then judge them at final screen scale; do not apply an automatic global multiplier. Track origin, action vector, onset and decay. Dark may use selective dark red on blood, eyes and meaningful scene/character details under its style guide. Keep quiet beats quiet, protect faces/grips/support/captions, and keep local action ink distinct from scene transitions. Stronger pigment never substitutes for correct contact or required poses.

Integrate this method with the large animated opening title, compact stylish readable captions, detailed scene direction, purposeful camera, visible atmosphere, clear narration and dramatic sound. The user's positive visual/method feedback does not resolve the current comparison's recorded registration, walking/contact or listening limits. Future actions still require actual adjacent-frame, normal-speed and physics review. Locking these instructions does not authorize a new animation or film.


## Whole-film coverage and consistent construction

Inventory every shot before generating: opening/reactions, dialogue/hand gestures, commands, animal movement, crowd/defender action, moving or breaking objects, quiet recovery and ending. Give each a visible-change plan, required complete drawings, timing, registered entry/exit and evidence. A proved fight does not carry an unanimated command or opening. An intentional stillness may be appropriate, such as a body after death; retain breathing/environmental or pigment motion only when physically and emotionally justified. Do not make a dead character breathe to satisfy a motion quota. Camera, splashes and line boil cannot replace required acting.

Use a shared style master and consistent pigment scale, silhouette detail, edges and costume masses across every shot. Review the opening, middle and ending together at delivery scale. Light/Dark share checked pose preparation and action phases. Dark has distinct oversized splash-built source forms and camera-led depth/reveal direction, rather than a palette-only adaptation of Light. Dark carries stronger calligraphic abstraction, pooled black and broken splash edges within the characters themselves, plus more expressive localized movement ink and darker scene design as the story calls for it. Keep faces, grips, ground support and captions clear; do not flood quiet intervals or equate dark stories with constant gore.

Original uses the same whole-pose planning, checked cropping, synchronization and connected acting. Allocate meaningful matching drawings and supported derived motion for the action, especially turns, walking, hands and contact recovery. Preserve30fps output and its fully Sumi-e material identity. Original uses20 designed character states/s with documented stillness; Light/Dark retain24fps/on-twos and shot-specific allowances. Do not smooth by warping limbs, inventing unsupported views or interpolating incompatible pictures. Count additional accepted drawings separately from output frames.

A whole-film coverage ledger should name each scene, motion phase, art master, accepted sources, entry/exit contact, and review status. Resolve inconsistent or incomplete scenes before presenting a fully updated film. This is an authored procedure; only actual evidence establishes whether the produced film follows it.
